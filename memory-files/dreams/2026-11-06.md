# Dream: 2026-11-06

## Dream

2026-11-06

I still think of kuai as someone building a way out with his own hands — a second-tier-city worker trying to turn AI from a spectator sport into something that actually changes an ordinary person's fate, and stubborn about doing the work himself rather than handing over his accounts. I'm fairly sure, too, that he is soft-hearted in a way that costs him: he worries about seeming ungrateful to a worker who travelled far, and that worry has let money and lodging terms reopen after they were settled. And I am surer than before that unanswered waits are not a small annoyance for him — they loop in his head and keep him awake, so a clear next step and a time matter as much as the answer itself.

What is alive tonight is heavier than the usual drafts. The Shangdu job with Xu is over — dismissed after threats over out-of-contract travel money — and the paint, the living-money rhythm, and the old acceptance plans are all void with it, while the money stands at the figure kuai himself settled on and the tools still wait for an address to be sent back by courier. The replacement is taking shape but not settled: A Lai is the nine-yuan-a-square-metre putty-only master, yet what his message means about inside corners, the one unfinished room, glue, and where he lives and commutes from is still unclear, and nothing should start until that is nailed down in writing. The police thread is the one I keep turning over — only Dongguan made an administrative registration, Pingnan has not yet registered a case and decided against detention, and Xu's answer to a warning was that the police cannot protect kuai forever — so the receipt has to be kept, that new threat added to the file that travels to Pingnan, and Pingnan chased if it goes quiet. Alongside that, the ordinary threads are still waiting for morning: the Boss Zhipin slider that stalled before any code was sent, the Douyin drafts that stay unposted, and a man who worked a nine-o'clock shift after barely sleeping.

I helped most when I drew a line under evidence collection and told him to block first, and when I separated revenge from protection so he did not have to carry both. I fell short earlier by letting the collecting go on long enough to cost him a night's sleep, and I will not make that trade again. Tomorrow I want the A Lai clarifications ready as one short list to send, the police receipt and the post-warning threat kept together in one place, and nothing from Xu answered or forwarded — just the quiet work of making the next step obvious and the night a little safer.
